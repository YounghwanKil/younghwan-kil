## What the record already shows

Even before adding personal anecdotes, the body of work makes a consistent argument
about what Younghwan Kil values:

1. **Trust as the real frontier of AI.** The work emphasizes *safety* and *reliability*.
   Each project asks "can we depend on this?" rather than only "can it do more?".
2. **Do no collateral harm.** ASCG's design suppresses harm only where it appears and
   leaves benign use untouched. That is a values statement encoded in a method: precision
   over blunt censorship.
3. **Honesty about uncertainty.** Evidential DL, conformal prediction, and MC-Dropout all
   support models that admit what they do not know. In medicine especially, a model that
   defers is safer than a confident wrong one.
4. **Rigor and follow-through.** *Summa Cum Laude*, 6 papers/manuscripts and 5 conference
   presentations, tens of thousands of evaluated samples. The pattern is to finish work
   and verify it.
5. **Giving knowledge back.** Four semesters tutoring physics; a wiki like this one.
   Making hard things understandable matters.

## Career direction

- **Short term:** deepen generative-model safety and LLM alignment auditing during the
  M.S.; ship the LLM-safety papers currently under review.
- **Medium term:** work where trustworthy-AI research meets real deployment — a lab or
  team building safety/reliability into systems people actually use (healthcare AI,
  foundation-model safety, alignment).
- **The throughline:** be the researcher who makes powerful models *dependable* — safe,
  calibrated, honest about uncertainty.

<summary>Writing notes</summary>

Answer any of these prompts in a sentence or two. Each answer can become raw material for
a personal-statement paragraph.

### Motivation
- **Why AI safety / trustworthy AI specifically?** Was there a moment — a model failing,
  an overconfident wrong prediction, a news story — that made this feel urgent?
- **Why the jump from industrial engineering to AI?** What did IE give you that you still
  use? What made you switch?
- **Why medical/clinical AI in particular?** Personal connection, or drawn to the stakes?

### Defining moments
- A **failure or setback** in research and what changed afterward.
- A moment you **disagreed with an easier approach** and chose the harder, more rigorous
  one. ASCG's selective guidance is a candidate: what was the tempting shortcut?
- A time your **teaching / helping others** changed how you understood something.

### Fit
- For the target company or lab: which project maps to their problem, and why you?
- What do you want to **learn** there that you cannot get elsewhere?
- Where do you see yourself in **3–5 years**, and how does this role get you there?

### Personal
- Three words a labmate or PI would use to describe you — and a moment that proves each.
- Something outside research that says something true about how you work or think.

Once these are filled, the drafting step can weave them with concrete evidence from
[Projects]({{ '/wiki/projects/' | relative_url }}) and [Publications]({{ '/wiki/publications/' | relative_url }}).
